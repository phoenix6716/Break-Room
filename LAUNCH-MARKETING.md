## Three short gameplay videos

Use real gameplay, readable on-screen text and a result you actually achieved. Keep answers and the result off screen until the reveal. Retain a clean original recording so you can reuse it on the social accounts you already have.

### 1. Time Sense — first video

On-screen opening: **How good is your sense of time?**

Voice/text:

> This game gives you a target time, then hides the timer.
> You have to stop it by instinct.
> Have a go with me.

Show the actual target from today’s Daily, press Start, then show the honest result. Let the challenge run for its real duration; don’t speed it up while asking viewers to judge the timing. Finish with:

> Better than me? Try today’s free game at breakroomdaily.com.

Caption:

> I made a game that tests your sense of time. Turns out mine needs work.
> Time Sense is one of 10 free Daily games at breakroomdaily.com.

Use “mine needs work” only if the recorded attempt supports it. If you do well: “I was surprisingly close. Can you beat it?”

### 2. Lowball — guessing hook

On-screen opening: **What would you guess this costs?**

Show one actual Daily item and the selected currency/region. Give viewers a moment to guess, enter your own estimate, and reveal the in-game reference price and score.

Finish:

> How close were you? Play today’s Lowball at breakroomdaily.com.

Caption:

> Apparently I have no idea what things cost.
> Your turn: today’s Lowball is free at breakroomdaily.com.

Use the self-deprecating line only for an actual poor guess. Prices are the game’s dated regional references, not a promise about every shop or today’s cheapest offer.

### 3. Laser — visual puzzle hook

On-screen opening: **Can you see where the mirrors go?**

Show today’s board with its actual mirror allowance. Pause briefly before your move. Show the beam and your genuine first-fire result. Finish:

> One of 10 free Daily games at breakroomdaily.com.

Caption:

> This looked easier before I pressed Fire.
> Have a go at today’s Laser: breakroomdaily.com/games/laser/

For a successful solve, use “Found the angle. Can you?” instead.

## What to learn from launch

Use the existing site traffic and account/payment dashboards, plus replies, rather than adding tracking software just for launch. Record visits, genuine sign-ups/purchases, which clips get clicks where link reporting is available, repeated interest in particular games, and recurring confusion. A useful first milestone is ten people giving specific feedback and some choosing to return the next day; this is a learning goal, not a traffic or sales forecast.

Keep ads and extra content promises out of this launch. Demand review activates after a verified paid monthly subscription. Author top-ups when active players’ unseen reserves need them, not because a new month begins.
