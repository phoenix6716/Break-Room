# Prior — early prototype review

Working name, 20 October 2026. The supplied original is archived unchanged. Its monetisation plug-in comments are not requested features.

Prior is worth developing: a five-choice chronology quiz is quicker and more immediately understandable than a five-card ordering puzzle. It overlaps with Stack Up's history content, so its identity should be the surprising connection between two events and a satisfying date reveal, rather than adding another timed sorting game.

The prototype has 65 events, five untimed Daily comparisons, free Unlimited and answer-encoding comparison codes. Across 365 generated days, 1,040 of 1,825 pairs had gaps of at least 100 years (median 119 years). Its eight-year minimum gap and unrestricted maximum gap often make it a very easy century-spotting exercise; it excludes close, interesting comparisons. It also loses active answers on refresh or mode change, saves completions without event snapshots, uses local midnight and example.com share links, and lacks the site's profile/cloud/social/accessibility contract.

Some claims need narrower scope: Bell's US patent rather than inventing the telephone; Wright Flyer flights rather than an unqualified worldwide first; the Human Genome Project completion milestone rather than a gapless genome; a specific public Web announcement rather than an ambiguous first website date; the Barclays Enfield dispenser rather than every possible cash-machine first. These are real weaknesses, not reasons to discard the core idea.

The implementation keeps five untimed Daily pairs and free endless practice. Daily progresses through one wider-gap, two medium and two close comparisons, with ten distinct events, no same-year ties and randomized sides. Related before/after milestones such as building/falling of the Berlin Wall are not paired together. The bank is expanded and precisely worded; dates/facts/source links are withheld until the answer. Readable archive cards, a timeline reveal and personal right/wrong feedback provide its visual identity within the canonical shell.

Native score stays correct choices out of five; Arcade score is correct × 20. Snapshots preserve historical scoring and reviewed state. Comparison uses the existing opt-in Friends feature instead of publishing answer codes. No timers, payments, sponsored questions or streak freezes are added.

Factual limitations: reference URLs accompany the events, but this environment has not retrieved their articles. Dates and milestone wording were checked during implementation; that is not a claim of live primary-source verification. Ambiguous approximately dated events (Niépce's earliest surviving photo and the Gutenberg Bible) were excluded. Complete the already deferred independent factual audit before launch. Gap bands are structural controls, not measured human difficulty.
